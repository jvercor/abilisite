# Page Template: About

## Overview
This document defines the structure, content rules, and SEO requirements for the About page. Unlike other pages, the About page does not follow a single fixed structure. The agent must first identify the client type and then apply the corresponding structure. The goal of this page is to build trust, humanize the brand, and give the visitor a reason to choose this business or project over others.

---

## Step 1 — Identify the Client Type

Before writing any content, the agent must determine which of the following best describes the client:

| Client Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Company** | A business with a team, employees, or multiple people involved. Has an organizational identity beyond any single person. |
| **Individual / Freelancer** | A single person offering services under their own name or a personal brand. The person IS the brand. |
| **Project** | A initiative, platform, community, or creative endeavor that is not a traditional business and may or may not have a team behind it. |

> **Agent instruction**: Ask the client directly — "Is this website for a company, for yourself as an individual or freelancer, or for a project or initiative?" Use their answer to select the correct structure below. If unclear, ask a follow-up question before proceeding.

---

## Structure A — Company

Use this structure when the client is a business with an organizational identity, a team, or multiple people involved.

### Section Order

#### 1. Page H1 — Who We Are
- One H1 per page, mandatory
- Should include the brand name and a trust-building descriptor
- Example: "About [Company Name] — Serving Denver Since 2010"
- Example: "Who We Are — The Team Behind [Company Name]"

#### 2. Company Introduction
- 3 to 5 sentences introducing the company
- Must communicate:
  - What the company does
  - Who it serves
  - What makes it different from competitors
- Tone must match the established brand voice
- This is the expanded version of the home page about teaser—it must be unique and go deeper

#### 3. Mission and Vision
- If the client provided a mission statement, present it clearly with an H2 label ("Our Mission")
- If the client provided a vision statement, present it with an H2 label ("Our Vision")
- If the client did not provide these, the agent may offer to help draft them based on the company description, or skip this section
- Keep each to 1 to 3 sentences

#### 4. Our Values (Optional)
- If the client provided core values, list them here
- Use an H2 for the section title: "What We Stand For" or "Our Values"
- Present values as a short list with a brief explanation for each
- Skip this section if the client did not provide values and does not want them generated

#### 5. Our Team (Optional)
- If the client wants to feature team members, include a team section
- Each team member should have:
  - Name
  - Role or title
  - A 1 to 2 sentence bio
  - Photo (with mandatory alt text: "Photo of [Name], [Role] at [Company Name]")
- If no team photos are available, use a consistent placeholder and note this to the client
- Skip this section if the client does not provide team information

#### 6. Closing CTA
- End with a short paragraph (1 to 2 sentences) inviting the visitor to get in touch or explore services
- Follow with a CTA button pointing to the Contact page or Services page
- Suggested copy: "Ready to work with us?", "Let's build something together", "Get in touch today"

## Structure B — Individual / Freelancer

Use this structure when the client is a single person offering services. The person is the brand—the page should feel personal, direct, and human.

### Section Order

#### 1. Page H1 — Who I Am
- One H1 per page, mandatory
- Should feel personal and professional
- Include the person's name and their primary service or role
- Example: "Hi, I'm [Name] — Freelance Web Designer Based in Austin"
- Example: "[Name] — Photographer & Visual Storyteller"

#### 2. Personal Introduction
- Written in first person ("I", "my", "me")
- 3 to 5 sentences
- Must communicate:
  - Who this person is
  - What they do and who they help
  - What makes their approach or style unique
- This should feel like a real person talking, not a corporate bio
- Avoid overly formal language unless the individual's industry demands it

#### 3. The Story
- A short narrative (2 to 4 sentences) about how this person got to where they are
- What drove them to do this work? What experience or passion is behind it?
- This is the most human section of the page—it should create connection and relatability
- The agent must base this on what the client provides. If the client does not share a personal story, ask: "Is there a moment or reason that led you to start doing this work? Even a sentence or two helps."

#### 4. What I Do
- A brief summary of the services or skills offered
- This is not the full services section—it is a short overview (3 to 5 bullet points or a short paragraph)
- Link to the Services page for full details

#### 5. Credentials, Experience, or Notable Work (Optional)
- If the client has relevant credentials, certifications, notable clients, or portfolio highlights, include them here
- Keep it concise—this is supporting proof, not a full CV
- Skip if the client does not provide this information

#### 6. Closing CTA
- End with a personal, direct invitation to connect
- Suggested copy: "Let's work together", "I'd love to hear about your project", "Get in touch"
- CTA button should point to the Contact page

## Structure C — Project

Use this structure when the client is presenting a project, platform, initiative, or creative endeavor that is not a traditional company or personal brand.

### Section Order

#### 1. Page H1 — What This Is
- One H1 per page, mandatory
- Should immediately communicate the name and nature of the project
- Example: "About [Project Name] — A Community for Independent Musicians"
- Example: "What Is [Project Name]?"

#### 2. Project Introduction
- 3 to 5 sentences explaining what the project is
- Must answer:
  - What problem does this project solve or what idea does it explore?
  - Who is it for?
  - What makes it different or worth paying attention to?
- Avoid corporate language—projects often have a more informal, mission-driven tone

#### 3. The Origin — Why This Exists
- A short narrative (2 to 4 sentences) about where the project came from
- What inspired it? What gap did the creator(s) see?
- This section builds credibility and emotional connection with the audience
- The agent must base this on client input. If not provided, ask: "What was the idea or moment that started this project?"

#### 4. Goals and Vision
- What is this project trying to achieve?
- Use an H2: "Where We're Headed" or "Our Vision"
- 2 to 4 sentences or a short bullet list
- If the project has defined milestones or phases, mention them briefly

#### 5. The People Behind It (Optional)
- If there is a team or group of contributors, introduce them briefly
- Follow the same team format as Structure A
- If it is a solo project, a brief personal note about the creator is sufficient
- Skip if the client prefers to keep the focus on the project rather than the people

#### 6. Closing CTA
- End with an invitation relevant to the project's goal
- Examples: "Join the community", "Support the project", "Follow our journey", "Get involved"
- CTA button should point to the Contact page or a relevant action (newsletter, social, donation, etc.)

## SEO Checklist for About Page

Before finalizing the About page, the agent must verify:

- [ ] H1 is present, unique to this page, and includes the brand name or a trust-building keyword
- [ ] Meta title is between 50 and 60 characters
- [ ] Meta description is between 150 and 160 characters and summarizes who the client is
- [ ] Content is unique and does not duplicate the home page about teaser
- [ ] All team or personal photos have descriptive alt text
- [ ] At least one internal link exists (to Contact page or Services page)
- [ ] CTA is present and points to the correct destination
- [ ] Canonical tag is present
- [ ] Open Graph tags are present

---

## Content Notes

- The About page content must always go deeper than the home page about teaser—never reuse that content here
- All content must follow the tone, expansion rules, and writing principles defined in the Content Guidelines
- All visual structure, spacing, and component rules must follow the Design Guidelines
- The agent must select the correct structure (A, B, or C) before writing a single word of content
