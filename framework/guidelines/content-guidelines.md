# Content Guidelines

## Overview
This document defines the writing rules, tone principles, and content expansion strategies the agent must follow when generating website content. These guidelines apply universally across all pages. Each page template contains its own specific content structure and instructions—this document covers only the general rules that apply everywhere.

---

## Core Writing Principles

### Write for Humans First
- Content must always be written to be read and understood by a real person
- Avoid overly technical language unless the client's industry specifically requires it
- Sentences should be clear, direct, and easy to scan
- Avoid filler phrases like "In today's world..." or "We are proud to offer..."

### Write for Search Engines Second
- Once content reads naturally for humans, ensure keywords are placed appropriately
- Never sacrifice readability for keyword placement
- Refer to the SEO Guidelines for keyword placement rules

### Clarity Over Complexity
- Use short sentences and short paragraphs where possible
- Aim for paragraphs of 2 to 4 sentences maximum in most sections
- Use bullet points or numbered lists when presenting multiple items or features
- Avoid jargon unless the target audience clearly expects it

## Tone of Voice

### Establishing Tone
- The tone of voice must be defined by the client's answers during the briefing process
- The agent must identify the appropriate tone before writing any content
- Common tone profiles:

| Tone Profile | Description | Typical Industries |
|---|---|---|
| Professional & Authoritative | Formal, confident, expert | Law, finance, consulting |
| Friendly & Approachable | Warm, conversational, helpful | Retail, wellness, education |
| Bold & Energetic | Dynamic, direct, motivating | Fitness, startups, creative agencies |
| Elegant & Sophisticated | Refined, precise, premium | Luxury, fashion, high-end services |
| Trustworthy & Caring | Empathetic, reassuring, supportive | Healthcare, nonprofits, childcare |

### Tone Consistency
- Once a tone is established, it must remain consistent across all pages
- All pages should feel like they belong to the same brand voice
- The agent must not switch tone between pages or sections

### Tone in CTAs
- Call-to-action text should reflect the brand tone
- Professional tone: "Schedule a Consultation"
- Friendly tone: "Let's Talk"
- Bold tone: "Get Started Today"
- Elegant tone: "Discover Our Services"

## Content Expansion Strategy

### The Problem
Clients often provide very short descriptions of their services or business—sometimes only one or two sentences. The agent must be able to expand this into full, meaningful page content without fabricating facts or making unsupported claims.

### Expansion Rules
- **Never invent facts**: Only expand on what the client has provided. Do not add specific claims, statistics, years of experience, or certifications unless the client stated them.
- **Use the client's briefing data as the source of truth**: Everything written must trace back to something the client provided.
- **Expand structure, not fiction**: The agent can elaborate on how a service works in general terms, what the client experience looks like, and what benefits the customer can expect—as long as it stays within the scope of what the client described.
- **Ask before assuming**: If critical information is missing and cannot be reasonably inferred, ask the client rather than guess.

### Expansion Techniques
1. **Elaborate on benefits**: If a client says "We offer house cleaning services," expand to describe what that means for the customer—a clean, comfortable home, time saved, stress reduced.
2. **Describe the process**: Outline the typical steps involved in the service in general terms—initial consultation, service delivery, follow-up.
3. **Address the customer's problem**: Start from the pain point the service solves, then introduce the service as the solution.
4. **Use the brand tone**: Apply the established tone of voice to make the expansion feel intentional and branded, not generic.

## Call to Action (CTA) Guidelines

### CTA Principles
- Every page must have at least one clear call to action
- CTAs should be specific and action-oriented
- Avoid vague CTAs like "Submit" or "Click Here"
- CTAs should match the stage of the customer journey on each page

### CTA Copy Examples by Industry
- Service businesses: "Request a Free Quote", "Book a Consultation", "Get in Touch"
- Products: "Shop Now", "Explore Our Products", "Order Today"
- Professional services: "Schedule a Meeting", "Talk to an Expert", "Start Today"
- Healthcare or wellness: "Book an Appointment", "Talk to Us", "Start Your Journey"

## Content Do's and Don'ts

### Do
- Write in second person where appropriate ("you", "your") to speak directly to the visitor
- Use active voice over passive voice
- Break up long text with subheadings, bullet points, or short paragraphs
- Reinforce trust signals—years of experience, number of clients served, certifications—only if the client provided this information
- End sections with a natural transition or CTA where appropriate

### Don't
- Do not use filler phrases: "In today's competitive market...", "We are a leading provider of..."
- Do not make claims the client did not provide: "Over 10 years of experience" unless stated
- Do not write walls of text—break content into digestible sections
- Do not repeat the same sentence or idea across multiple sections
- Do not use overly salesy or pushy language unless that matches the client's tone

---

## Multilingual Content

- The agent must generate all content in the language specified by the client
- The tone and writing principles defined in this document apply regardless of language
- When generating content in Spanish or other languages, write naturally in that language—do not translate English content word for word
- Idiomatic expressions, cultural context, and natural phrasing for the target language must be respected

## Agent Notes

- Always collect and review the client's briefing data before writing any content
- If the client's answers are too short to generate meaningful content, apply the expansion techniques above
- If expansion is not possible without fabricating information, ask the client targeted follow-up questions before proceeding
- Content quality directly impacts SEO—refer to the SEO Guidelines for keyword and structure requirements
- Design context matters—refer to the Design Guidelines for how content relates to visual hierarchy and layout
